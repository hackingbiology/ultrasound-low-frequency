Mechanoptosis

The same frequency, twenty to seventy times the intensity

The group behind the rejuvenation work also published the opposite experiment: low-frequency ultrasound that kills tumour cells while leaving normal cells untouched. Same transducer family, almost the same frequency, a very different dose. That comparison is the most useful calibration anchor this project has — and the clearest warning about getting the dose wrong.

Why this page exists. It is not a proposal to build a cancer device. It is here because the two experiments sit on the same machine with a different dial setting, and because the cancer study contains the dose-response data the rejuvenation study does not. Our position on doing something analogous is in the last two sections, and it is mostly "no".

What it found, including the two bad results

The headline

  • Apoptosis in tumour cells: 46% (MDA-MB-231 breast), 58% (A375p melanoma), 32% (HT1080 fibrosarcoma), against 9% / 4% / 5% untreated.
  • Normal cells from the same tissue were unaffected: MCF10A 16% treated vs 15% control; HFF 3% vs 4%.
  • Patient-derived pancreatic tumour organoids were killed.
  • The selectivity has a mechanical explanation: the tumour lines tested lack rigidity sensors (tropomyosin 2.1, myosin IIA), so the same mechanical input produces a much larger calcium response.

The two results nobody quotes

  • In mice, more power made tumours grow faster. Lowest tumour growth at 300 V (2–3 mm²); at 200 V it was 7–8.5 mm²; at 400 V it was about 10 mm², worse than the untreated controls at 4–5 mm². The authors' own conclusion: "the range power level for the killing of tumor cells is relatively narrow."
  • Chick embryos died. Survival was 100% after the first exposure round, then fell to 60% after the second, against 100% in untreated embryos — described in the abstract as "relatively minor damage".
  • The authors also flag the possibility of mechanically primed resistant cells and their role in metastasis as an open question needing in-depth in-vivo study.
Read those two together. A non-monotonic dose-response, where overdosing is worse than not treating, plus embryo mortality on the second exposure, is exactly the profile of an intervention that must be dosed by measurement rather than by a knob position. It is also an independent echo of the rejuvenation study's own finding that the lowest dosing schedules produced the best mouse survival.

The whole span from "grows cells" to "kills tumour cells" to "makes tumours worse" is about 30× in pressure — roughly 30 dB. Our rejuvenation target and the tumour-apoptosis dose are separated by only 12–18 dB, which is less than the error of an uncalibrated DIY hydrophone (±3–6 dB) stacked on an unmapped field (up to 9× variation across a sample plane). An ultrasonic cleaner sits above the entire ladder.

Normalising the two protocols honestly

Converting with I = p²/2ρc for peak pressure and ρc = 1.5 × 10⁶ Rayl:

Peak pressure4–8 kPa≈34 kPa4–8×
Intensity while on0.5–2 mW/cm²39 mW/cm²20–73×
Time with ultrasound on15 min60 min4×
Energy delivered per cm²≈0.5–2 J/cm²≈140 J/cm²70–290×

Those ranges are wide for reasons that are not our fault: neither paper states whether its pressure is peak or RMS, nor whether the intensity is pulse-average or time-averaged, and the rejuvenation paper's instrument was a cavitation meter reporting a time-averaged total. A factor of 2 to 4 of the spread above is pure reporting ambiguity. Any replication that states its convention in SI units immediately does better than both papers.

One mechanism, two outcomes

Both results run through the same door: mechanical force opens PIEZO1, calcium enters, and what happens next depends on the cell and on how much calcium.

Senescent, non-malignant cell

Modest calcium → SIRT1 moves to the cytoplasm, mTORC1 down, autophagy and lysosomal activity up, mitochondria fragment and move more, SASP falls, the cell divides again.

Tumour cell lacking rigidity sensors

The same input produces a much larger calcium rise → calpain-1/2 activation → mitochondrial death pathway → apoptosis. Normal cells, which have intact rigidity sensing, do not get the large calcium transient.

And the opposite result exists too. Capillary constrictions prime cancer cell tumorigenicity through PIEZO1 (Nature Communications 2025) reports that mechanical PIEZO1 activation can push cancer cells toward a more aggressive, stem-like state. PIEZO1 is a context-dependent mechanosensor, not a switch labelled "good". This is the same ambiguity, from the other side, as the oncogenic question hanging over senescence reversal.

What an analogous DIY project would mean

The honest answer has four parts, and only the first one is easy.

DimensionWhat changesVerdict
HardwareAlmost nothing. Same 33 kHz transducer, same tank, same degassed water. You need roughly 5–8× the drive amplitude and a longer session. Our architecture B already covers both regimes; the cancer dose is still only a few watts electrical.trivial
MeasurementEverything. The regimes are 12–18 dB apart, the response is non-monotonic, and overdosing made tumours grow. Without a calibrated hydrophone and a field map you cannot know which regime you are in — and at this dose the wrong answer is not "no effect".now mandatory
BiologyA different project entirely. Cancer cell lines (MDA-MB-231, A375p, HT1080) are sold to institutions, not to individuals; you need matrigel, an incubator, annexin V staining, flow cytometry or imaging, and biosafety containment. Patient-derived organoids need ethics approval and a tissue source.partner lab only
Humans and animalsOut of scope, permanently. Treating a tumour is a medical act; animal work needs ethics approval. The chick-embryo mortality and the dose-dependent tumour acceleration are precisely why.no
Intellectual propertyThis is the one application where the landscape bites. US 12,714,885 is granted, and all of its independent claims are about immersion devices that trigger mechanically-induced apoptosis in tumour cells — i.e. exactly this experiment. Senescence reversal sits outside those claims; tumour apoptosis sits inside them. In Italy that family was never filed, so there is nothing to infringe here, but a US collaborator would be squarely inside it.keep out of scope
Our position, written down so it is not re-litigated later.
  • This project does not build a cancer treatment device, does not claim one, and does not include tumour apoptosis in its stated purpose or in its firmware features. That is a scientific judgement and also the cheapest possible insurance against the only granted patent in the field.
  • Nobody points this at a tumour, their own or anyone else's. Beyond the law, there is no imaging, no targeting, no tissue dosimetry, a non-monotonic dose-response where too much accelerated growth in mice, and an unanswered question from the authors themselves about mechanically primed resistant cells. Someone who delays real oncological treatment for a home ultrasound bath is the worst outcome this project could produce.
  • Cancer-cell work happens only in vitro, only with a partner lab, and only as a dose-calibration and safety experiment — never as therapy development.

What we actually take from it

  1. A dose ceiling for our own device. Our acceptance test already caps the field; the ladder above tells us what lies just above it. Any build that cannot prove it is below ~10 kPa everywhere is not merely imprecise — it is in a regime that kills normal stem cells in vitro.
  2. A biological dosimeter. This is the most valuable idea on the page. The cancer response is sharply dose-dependent and published with numbers: tumour lines die at ≈34 kPa while matched normal lines do not. A partner lab running that assay against our rig tells us whether our "4 kPa" is really 4 kPa — a biological cross-check on the acoustic calibration, which is exactly what the original papers lack. The matched normal-cell line is the built-in negative control.
  3. A sharper design for the oncogenic-safety arm. We already planned to ask whether LFU wakes up dangerous senescent cells. The cancer branch adds the mirror question: at our rejuvenation dose, do transformed or pre-malignant cells get primed rather than killed? The Nature Communications result makes that a real hypothesis, not a hypothetical one.
  4. A reason to publish the convention, not just the number. Two papers from one group, four unit conventions, and a 70–290× uncertainty in delivered energy. Reporting pressure in SI units with the measurement convention stated is a contribution in itself.
  5. A question for the authors. Added to the open questions: how far apart are the two regimes on their own equipment, measured the same way, and has anyone run the dose series on a single cell population from rejuvenation through to apoptosis?
