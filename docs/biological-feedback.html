Measurement

Analyze biological feedback

Building the device is the easy half. The hard half is knowing whether anything happened. This page maps what the original work measured against what a self-experimenter can measure, names the tests, the kits and the Italian labs, and shows why most inflammatory markers are useless in an n-of-1 unless you repeat them.

There is no gold-standard blood test for cellular senescence. The 2026 compendium of circulating senescence biomarkers (Olinger & Basisty, Ageing Res Rev) puts GDF15, Activin A, IL-6, MMP1 and MMP7 at the top, and says plainly that it is currently impossible to tell which tissue they come from or to separate senescence from other causes. So: a panel plus physical function, never a single number.

What this table tells us. Three things stand out. First, the mechanism itself (cell-cycle re-entry) is invisible from blood: we can only see its consequences, above all the SASP. Second, the human trial's own primary endpoint is functional, not molecular — muscle strength and walking — so a biohacker measuring grip strength and a 6-minute walk is measuring the same class of thing as UT Health San Antonio. Third, nobody has published the delivered dose for humans, which is the one column where an open project can beat the original.

Tier 2 · With a partner research lab

  • p16INK4a + p21 + cGAS RT-qPCR on sorted CD3+ T cells — the closest thing to a "senescent burden" blood test (Liu & Sharpless 2009: ~10× rise over six decades; exercise lowers it). 10–15 mL EDTA, CD3 microbeads, RNA, TaqMan. Consumables roughly €40–90 per sample. Any university molecular-biology lab can do it.
  • Flow-cytometry SA-β-gal on PBMCs (CellEvent Senescence Green, ~$350 for 50 tests, or SPiDER-βGal) with surface markers: the senescent-like fraction is highest in CD8+ cells. Needs fresh PBMCs within hours.
  • SASP multiplex: Luminex/MSD custom panel or Olink Target 96 Inflammation (92 proteins from 1 µL). Decisive advantage for n-of-1: all pre and post samples run on the same plate, which removes inter-assay variance. Olink's practical minimum is a full plate, so it only makes sense with several participants.
  • RNA-seq on PBMC/CD3+ scored with SenMayo (125-gene senescence set) — the home-brew analogue of the "RNA-seq" in the UT protocol.
  • EPIC v2 methylation array at an EU service, clocks computed with open software (DunedinPACE, pyaging). This is the only serious way to use clocks in an n-of-1, because you can run technical duplicates and put pre/post samples on the same chip.

Epigenetic clocks are not an 8-week endpoint. Their technical reproducibility is good, but their short-term biological stability is ICC 0.4–0.7 — a meal or acute stress moves them. For scale: two years of caloric restriction in CALERIE shifted DunedinPACE by 2–3%. Use clocks as context, never as the primary outcome.

Urine, honestly

There is no validated urinary SASP panel. 8-oxo-dG and 8-oxo-Gsn measure whole-body oxidative repair turnover, not senescence, with strong diurnal variation and ELISA-vs-mass-spectrometry disagreement. F2-isoprostanes are a solid oxidative-stress marker with no direct link to senescent burden. Collect urine only if a partner lab has LC-MS/MS; otherwise it does not deserve budget. It is the weakest link in the protocol.

Why repetition matters more than the marker

For a marker with analytical variation CVA and within-person biological variation CVI, a change only counts if it exceeds the reference change value: RCV ≈ 2.77 × √(CVA² + CVI²). Averaging k samples before and k after shrinks it by √(1/k) — with k = 3 the requirement drops by 42%.

Read this against the expected effect size. Interventions with real senolytic or lifestyle effects move single analytes by 10–30% in studies with dozens of subjects. So in an n-of-1: GDF-15 is the only credible primary endpoint; IL-6 and hs-CRP alone cannot support a conclusion; everything else is exploratory. Report every marker as "Δ% with its RCV beside it". "−18% with RCV ±12.5%" is a result. "IL-6 −25% with RCV ±62%" is not.

Timing: two traps specific to ultrasound

1 · The acute SASP may go the wrong way

Low-intensity pulsed ultrasound has been shown to increase SASP secretion acutely (ROS → p38-NF-κB), recruiting macrophages that then clear senescent cells. A blood draw a few hours after a session can therefore show inflammation rising even if the chronic effect is a fall.

2 · p16 in T cells can be falsely high

T-cell activation induces p16 in a calcium-dependent way — and calcium influx via Piezo1 is exactly the proposed LFU mechanism. An acute p16 rise may mean activation, not senescence.

Rule: chronic-effect samples are drawn at least 48–72 hours after the last session, always at the same interval. Optionally add an explicitly exploratory acute series (pre-session, +2 h, +24 h) on one single session.

And remember the bath itself. Forty-five minutes in 35 °C water is an intervention: haemodynamics, IL-6, sleep and mood all respond. The UT trial has a sham arm for precisely this reason. An n-of-1 has no sham, so the washout phase is the closest substitute, and alternating "bath without ultrasound" sessions is better still.

The n-of-1 protocol

  1. Standardise everything before you measure anything. Same time of day (07:30–09:00), 12 h fasting, no intense exercise for 48 h (GDF-15 rises 4–5× after a marathon), no infection, vaccination or surgery in the previous 4 weeks, no alcohol for 48 h, same tube type and same lab every time, centrifuged within 1–3 h, aliquoted and frozen at −80 °C.
  2. Three baselines, not one (weeks −4, −2, 0), each with the full functional battery repeated three times. This is also how you estimate your own biological variation.
  3. Eight weeks of exposure, matching the UT trial: 45 min, 3×/week. Log every session with its measured acoustic dose.
  4. Three post samples (weeks 8, 9, 10), the first 72 h after the last session.
  5. Washout re-test at week 16. A return toward baseline is the strongest evidence obtainable without a sham arm.
  6. Freeze spare aliquots — your personal biobank costs nothing and lets you re-run everything in one batch later, which is what removes inter-assay noise.
  7. Pre-declare one primary endpoint (proposed: plasma GDF-15, mean of 3 pre vs mean of 3 post) and label everything else exploratory. Publish raw data and code so that many identical n-of-1 runs can later be pooled.

Panels and costs

Minimum panel — "don't do less than this"

Per time point (3 pre + 3 post):

  1. CBC with differential (→ NLR) — €4–10
  2. hs-CRP — €5–15
  3. High-sensitivity IL-6 — €17–100
  4. GDF-15 — the primary endpoint — €30–80 (or freeze plasma for a batch ELISA)
  5. HbA1c, glucose, insulin (HOMA-IR) — ~€25
  6. Creatinine/eGFR + cystatin C, liver panel, albumin, lipids — ~€40
  7. Extra frozen aliquots — free, and the highest-value item on the list

Once at baseline: CMV IgG, protein electrophoresis, TSH, vitamin D, ferritin, homocysteine. Plus the functional battery.

Total ≈ €900–2,400 for six time points, plus a dynamometer.

"Done well" panel — with a partner lab

  • SASP multiplex (GDF15, Activin A, TNFR1, OPN, FAS, CCL3, IL-15, IL-6, MMP-2/7/9, PAI-1, RAGE, CCL18, TIMP1, IGFBP2) — all samples on one plate
  • p16 + p21 + cGAS RT-qPCR on sorted CD3+ cells
  • Flow cytometry: CD3/4/8/CD45RA/CCR7/CD28/CD57/KLRG1 + SA-β-gal on fresh PBMCs
  • RNA-seq on PBMC/CD3+ with SenMayo scoring
  • EPIC v2 methylation in duplicate, same chip → DunedinPACE, PC clocks, immune fractions
  • cfDNA / cf-mtDNA (exploratory, very strict pre-analytics)
  • DEXA at baseline and week 8, as in the trial (~€60–120 privately)

Oncological safety monitoring

The premise cannot be argued away. An intervention that returns senescent cells to proliferation removes, in principle, an anti-tumour barrier. The mouse study found no tumours over 300+ days, but there is no human data, and no blood test can rule out cancer. Tumour-marker panels (CEA, CA125, CA19-9) are explicitly not recommended as screening in people without symptoms: the positive predictive value is very low and false positives lead to invasive procedures. Note too that GDF-15 itself is elevated in many cancers, so an unexpected rise is a clinical question, not an "aging" reading.
